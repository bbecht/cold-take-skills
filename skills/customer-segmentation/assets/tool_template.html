How do the two models work?

What does logistic regression do?

It gives every attribute a weight, adds the weights up, and turns the total into a probability between 0% and 100%. It assumes every effect runs in a straight line: more of something is always better, or always worse. Its strength is that you can read it. Its weakness is that it misses curves, like a sweet spot in company size.

What does random forest do?

It grows hundreds of decision trees. Each tree asks a chain of yes or no questions, like "Over 200 employees?" then "Software?", and sees different slices of the data. The forest's probability is the share of trees that vote "win". It catches curves and combinations. It is harder to read, and its percentages bunch toward the middle.

Why run both?

They fail in different ways. The model that ranks unseen accounts better sets the probability. The other is a second opinion. When they disagree on an attribute, the effect is curved or conditional. When they disagree on an account, check it by hand.

When should I not trust this?

When the check score is under 0.60. When the data covers fewer than 300 closed deals. When your market, product or pricing changed since those deals closed. And always remember the models learn only from accounts your team chose to work. They cannot score a type of account you never tried.
